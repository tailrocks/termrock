# 0309 — Danger is quiet by default

The permission prompt — the highest-risk surface in the library — painted an
`AccentRail` in the risk role down its left edge and framed the whole request
in it. A red box around everything makes the operator read nothing, which is
the opposite of what a trust surface is for.

The consensus recorded as decision D1 in
`docs/design/termrock-component-audit-2026-08.md` (shadcn, Amp, Linear, Grok)
is that danger lives on the **confirm button**, not on the container.

## Behavior change

`PermissionPrompt` drops the severity rail. Its container is neutral chrome —
`Border`, or `BorderFocused` when the surface owns focus — and the risk is
carried by:

- the title's risk glyph and word (`⚠ high`, `⛔ critical`), already present;
- the destructive confirm chip, painted in `Role::Danger` by the action bar;
- the safety lines the frame keeps regardless of the information budget
  (egress destination, `reversible: NO`, the warning).

The prompt also gains its two reclaimed columns back — the rail was one cell
plus its gap.

## New surface

```rust
use termrock::widgets::{DangerChrome, PermissionPrompt};

// Default: quiet.
let prompt = PermissionPrompt::new(&system);

// Opt in for the irreversible-of-irreversible case.
let shouting = PermissionPrompt::new(&system).danger_chrome(DangerChrome::Loud);
```

`DangerChrome::Loud` restores a `Role::Danger` border, and only for a
destructive risk. It has to be asked for; `Quiet` is the default so that the
loud form still means something when it appears.

## Consumer edits

- A host asserting the danger-colored cell at the prompt's top-left gets
  `BorderFocused` now; assert on the title glyph or the confirm chip instead,
  or opt into `DangerChrome::Loud`.
- Layout that accounted for the rail's column: the content rect is two cells
  wider.
