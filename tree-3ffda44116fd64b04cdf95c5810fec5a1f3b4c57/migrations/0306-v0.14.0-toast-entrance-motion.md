# 0306 — Toasts fade in, errors do not, success acknowledges once

A toast stack had no entrance at all: every toast appeared at full strength in
one frame, including the ones that should have been quiet, and none of them
distinguished "this just arrived" from "this has been here".

## The per-kind rule

`ToastState::paint_alpha(tick, kind, policy)` returns the paint alpha:

| Kind | Behavior |
|------|----------|
| `Error` | **No animation, ever.** Full strength on the first frame |
| `Success` | Entrance fade, then one dip back to full — an acknowledgement |
| everything else | Entrance fade over `TOAST_ENTER_MS` (120 ms), then hold |

**Errors never fade** because a failure must be readable the instant it lands;
fading one in delays the single message that cannot wait. That is a deliberate
exception to the overlay contract, not an oversight.

**Success dips rather than brightens.** Full brightness is the ceiling, so an
acknowledging "pulse" can only go down and come back. The curve starts and ends
at `1.0` — a breathe would end at its trough and snap when it stopped.
Amplitude is capped at `style::AMBIENT_PEAK`, so it whispers.

## Reduced motion

- `Off` — every kind lands instantly at `1.0`.
- `Basic` — keeps the ≤ 120 ms entrance (SoT §3 allows short transitions) but
  **not** the ambient success pulse.

## New public surface

- `widgets::TOAST_ENTER_MS`, `widgets::TOAST_SUCCESS_PULSE_MS`
- `ToastState::shown_at() -> Option<Instant>`
- `ToastState::paint_alpha(FrameTick, ToastKind, MotionPolicy) -> f32`

## Where the time comes from

`ToastStack::paint` is unchanged — no new parameter. Frame time arrives through
`DesignSystem::at(tick)` (migration 0299). A host that never calls `at` gets
full-strength toasts, which is the correct settled state.

## Still to come

Exit fades and the 100 ms stack-reflow tween are **not** in this boundary: both
need the queue to hold an entry past its TTL and to carry a per-id animator,
which changes queue semantics rather than paint. Tracked in `plans/README.md`
under plan 014.
